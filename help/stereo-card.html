Stereo Card

Lays out each pair as a stereo card: two prints side by side on a card of a genuine format, behind windows of a period shape. It ends a branch: its cards leave the workflow through Export… and Print…, on its card and in its dialog. Exported as PDF, the card and its words are drawn, and the prints embedded at their own resolution.

Formats

Chosen on its card, or under Dimensions in its dialog:

  • Holmes-Bates — the standard 3½ × 7 in card for the Holmes–Bates stereoscope, laid out after Keystone's.
  • Thorpe — Thorpe's Digital Stereoview Standard: a 4 × 6 in photographic print.
  • Phone cards — the size and shape of a telephone's screen held sideways, at life size, with margins wide enough to read the words.
  • OWL — 178 × 85 mm, for the London Stereoscopic Company's OWL viewer.
  • 6 × 13 cm — with prints 5 cm square.

The card

The same for every pair, and saved with the workflow:

  • Stock — white, cream, buff, yellow, orange, grey or black. Words are printed in dark brown, or in gold on black.
  • Texture — a photograph of genuine card stock, laid over the card in place of its colour.
  • Window — arched top (the default), square or oval; a corner radius for square and arched windows, and a rise for the arch.

Words

A card bears words only from a Text & Order node before it; without one, the Text settings are disabled and the card is bare. Like the cards of the period, it carries no title.

  • Caption — beneath the right print (the default) or the same beneath both, aligned Left, Centre or Right (the default).
  • Number — Top Centre at the head of the card, a corner at the head or foot of either end, or None (the default). It is set at the caption's size, hung from the upper edge or stood on the lower; where the margin is narrower than the figures, it will lie over the window.
  • Ends — the texts along the card's ends: Turned (the default), running up the left end and down the right, as the publishers printed them; or Upright, level, read top to bottom, and set smaller where needed so the longest fits — a long text makes a tall, narrow column.

Framing, for each pair

Drag the picture to frame it within the window; the framing keeps to the window's shape.

Life Size

With Life Size checked, in the dialog's header, the card is shown at its true size, to be free-viewed as a card held in the hand. The size comes from the display's report of its dimensions, and is offered only where that report is credible. A phone card is instead shown pixel for pixel (Actual Pixels), as the telephone will show it. Drag or scroll the card should the window be too small. It is the operator's preference, kept for every card, and not saved with the collection.
